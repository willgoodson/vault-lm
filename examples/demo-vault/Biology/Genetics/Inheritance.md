# Inheritance

Gregor Mendel's pea-plant experiments (1850s–60s) showed that traits are passed on in discrete units, which we now call genes (see [[DNA and Genes]]).

- **Alleles** are versions of a gene. You inherit one allele from each parent.
- A **dominant** allele (B) shows its trait even with one copy; a **recessive** allele (b) only shows with two copies (bb).
- **Genotype** is the allele pair (BB, Bb, bb); **phenotype** is the visible trait.

## Punnett square: Bb × Bb
|   | B | b |
|---|---|---|
| **B** | BB | Bb |
| **b** | Bb | bb |

Result: 3 : 1 ratio of dominant to recessive phenotype (1 BB : 2 Bb : 1 bb).

## Mendel's laws
1. **Segregation**: the two alleles separate when gametes form.
2. **Independent assortment**: genes on different chromosomes are inherited independently.
