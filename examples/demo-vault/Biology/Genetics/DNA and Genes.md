# DNA and Genes

**DNA** (deoxyribonucleic acid) is a double helix of two strands held together by base pairs: **A–T** and **C–G**. Each strand is a chain of nucleotides (a sugar, a phosphate and a base).

A **gene** is a stretch of DNA that codes for a protein. Humans have roughly 20,000 protein-coding genes spread across 23 pairs of chromosomes.

## From gene to protein
1. **Transcription** (in the nucleus): RNA polymerase copies a gene into messenger RNA (mRNA).
2. **Translation** (at the ribosome): the mRNA is read three bases (one **codon**) at a time, and transfer RNA brings the matching amino acid.

This flow, DNA → RNA → protein, is called the **central dogma** of molecular biology.

## Mutations
A mutation is a change in the DNA sequence. A *point mutation* swaps one base; an *insertion* or *deletion* can shift the reading frame and change every codon after it. See [[Inheritance]] for how genes pass between generations.
