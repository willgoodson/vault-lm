# Cell Structure

All living things are made of cells, the smallest unit that can carry out the processes of life. **Cell theory** states that every organism is made of one or more cells, that the cell is the basic unit of life, and that new cells come only from existing cells.

## Prokaryotes and eukaryotes
- **Prokaryotic cells** (bacteria and archaea) have no nucleus. Their DNA sits in a region called the nucleoid, and they are usually 1–10 µm across.
- **Eukaryotic cells** (plants, animals, fungi, protists) keep their DNA inside a membrane-bound **nucleus** and contain other membrane-bound [[Organelles]]. They are typically 10–100 µm across.

## Shared features
Every cell has:
1. A **plasma membrane**, a phospholipid bilayer that controls what enters and leaves (see [[Cell Membrane Transport]]).
2. **Cytoplasm**, the fluid interior where most reactions happen.
3. **Ribosomes**, which build proteins from the instructions in DNA (see [[DNA and Genes]]).
4. **DNA** as the genetic material.

## Plant vs. animal cells
| Feature | Plant cell | Animal cell |
|---|---|---|
| Cell wall | Yes (cellulose) | No |
| Chloroplasts | Yes | No |
| Central vacuole | One large | Small or none |
| Shape | Fixed, boxy | Flexible, rounded |
