# Organelles

Organelles are the specialised compartments of a eukaryotic cell (see [[Cell Structure]]).

- **Nucleus**: stores DNA and controls the cell's activity. The nucleolus inside it makes ribosomes.
- **Mitochondria**: the site of aerobic [[Cellular Respiration]], producing most of the cell's ATP. They have their own DNA, evidence that they descend from ancient bacteria (the endosymbiotic theory).
- **Chloroplasts**: found in plants and algae; they carry out [[Photosynthesis]]. Like mitochondria, they have a double membrane and their own DNA.
- **Endoplasmic reticulum (ER)**: *rough ER* is studded with ribosomes and makes proteins for export; *smooth ER* makes lipids and detoxifies.
- **Golgi apparatus**: modifies, sorts and packages proteins into vesicles.
- **Lysosomes**: contain digestive enzymes that break down waste and worn-out organelles.
- **Vacuoles**: store water and nutrients; in plants the large central vacuole keeps the cell firm (turgor pressure).

> [!tip] Memory hook
> Think of the cell as a factory: the nucleus is the head office, ribosomes are the workers, the ER is the assembly line, the Golgi is shipping, and mitochondria are the power plant.
